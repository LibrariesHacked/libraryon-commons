Many [libraries across the country](/libraries) offer free sessions that help people build their **digital literacy**.

They provide clear, easy-to-follow support to help people of all ages feel safe and confident using modern technology.

This important life skill helps you access convenient services, stay connected to others, enjoy entertainment and live independently.

> "At Tablet Tasters I take along a list of questions and the staff answer them all. It’s given me the confidence to try more with technology."

Mr Ali

Customer, Southend Libraries

At library **digital skills sessions** you can get help with things like:

-   Using your smartphone or tablet for the first time
-   Connecting with family online
-   Accessing digital content through the library
-   Learning how to be safe from online scams and threats
-   Teaching children how to use devices responsibly.

**Did you know?**

Digital skills sessions go by different names at different libraries.

At your library they might be called something like:

-   Digital Hour
-   Digital Drop-in
-   Digital Champions
-   Silver Surfers
-   Tablet Tasters
-   Get Online At Your Library.

## Skills and volunteering for young people

![A teenage boy coding on a laptop in a library.](/uploads/archive/845be4d6a13f31eec5b660e220054013817a22470d9f83fe55d9cc78235e7106.jpg)

Code Clubs in libraries offer ways for children to learn – and teach – digital skills among peers. Photo: Leamington Library by LibraryOn

[Code Club](https://codeclub.org/en/) is a nationwide initiative offering free after-school coding sessions for children.

You can find local clubs in schools and libraries around the country. They help young people enjoy technology, create digital projects and develop skills like problem-solving and communication.

Teaching others is a great way to build skills too, and there are often volunteering opportunities at libraries.

## Questions about digital skills in libraries

### Who are library digital skills sessions aimed at?

Some sessions are aimed at adults who might not be very familiar with technology. Others are aimed at all ages.

See if a [library near you](/libraries) offers digital skills sessions and, if it does, staff will be able to advise you.

### How can the library help me improve my digital skills?

Libraries all over the country offer drop-in sessions where you can bring your device and get help from a volunteer.

If you don't have a device you can often use one of the library's computers.

### Do I need to be a member to get digital skills help at the library?

It might vary. But many digital skills drop-in sessions will be open to non-library members.

### Do I need to book to attend a library digital skills session?

This might vary by library. But many library digital skills sessions tend to be drop-in, which means you don't need to book.

### Do I need to bring my own device to a digital skills session at the library?

If you want to learn how to get the most out of your device, you can usually take it along and a volunteer will help you.

But you don't have to take a device to ask for advice or support.

### Can I use a computer or the internet at the library?

Yes. Lots of public libraries offer free access to computers and wi-fi.

Staff can help you get started if you’re new to using them. There might be a time limit on how long your computer session will last.
