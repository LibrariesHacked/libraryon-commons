# Makerspaces in libraries

## Making and creating for all ages

The library is a great way to **get creative with new technology** without spending lots of money. And all ages are welcome.

Libraries with makerspaces allow you to try out things like **3D printing** and **podcasting**. Code clubs for young people are also a common sight, helping with STEAM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts and Mathematics) education.

![A 3D printer in a library makerspace prints a small golden arch.](/uploads/archive/1672ed7c61e84f18d9f0e7cbcc2bff8f6d61753351ea427f54b402fd0cf52b23.jpg)

The Hive at Darlington Library offers 3D printing, animation, sewing machines, laser cutters and more. Image: Darlington Libraries

Playing with technology is a great way to make it feel less intimidating, whether it’s a robotics system or a digital camera. Check out [libraries near you](/libraries) to see what's on offer.

**Did you know?**

‘Makerspace’ is just one name for places where people can make and create. Other names for them include:

- Creatorspace
- Idea lab
- Fab lab
- Makery
- Imagination Station.

## Find your voice at the library

**Podcasting** is a great way to go deep on a topic you’re really passionate about and share your take with the world.

Instead of buying expensive equipment, it’s worth checking whether a library near you has podcasting gear and space you could use.

Libraries that offer podcast production tools also tend to put on events and courses to help you get to grips with them.

## Questions about using makerspaces in libraries

### Who are makerspaces for?

Most makerspaces in libraries have been designed to appeal to people of all ages. Some of the technology might only be suitable for adults though.

People use makerspaces to do things related to their hobbies or their work, such as if they've started their own business. They might use a makerspace to:

- Create or edit content, such as photography or video
- Make 3D-printed prototypes of products they're working on
- Practise traditional arts and crafts like woodworking, sewing or painting
- Learn about creating through coding, electronics and robotics.

Every makerspace is different. If a library near you has one, it's a good idea to drop by and chat to the staff to learn more.

### What will I find in a library makerspace?

Things you might find in a library makerspace include:

- 3D printers
- Photo, video and animation production equipment
- Podcasting studios or equipment
- Sewing machines
- Laser cutters
- Virtual Reality headsets
- Robotics systems
- Code clubs
- Woodworking or metalworking equipment
- Screen printing tools.

### Can I use library makerspaces to work on my own projects?

Yes! Whether you’re a beginner or an experienced maker, you’ll be welcome to use a library makerspace to work on your projects.

### Do I need to pay to use a library makerspace?

It varies. Some might offer free access to equipment but ask you to pay for materials.

Also, some library makerspaces might offer free introductory and taster sessions. It’s best to check with the library first.

### Do I need to be a library member to use a library makerspace?

If you’re not a member already you might need to become a library member to use a library makerspace.

Remember: it’s **free** to join the library... and you might find a book to help with your project!
